About Me

Hi, I'm Harry

I'm a software developer based in the UK. I've been running this site in one form or another since 2006 — it started as a place to dump links and has slowly turned into something more like an actual blog. Still not entirely sure what it is. That's fine by me.

My main interests are old Mac OS X, film photography, and cooking. They don't have much in common except that I find all three more satisfying than their modern equivalents. There's a theme there if you squint.

The Computer

My main machine is a 2009 MacBook Pro running Mac OS X Snow Leopard 10.6.8. It has 8GB of RAM and an aftermarket SSD, which makes it feel genuinely quick for almost everything I do day to day.

I know that sounds eccentric. It isn't, really. Snow Leopard is fast, stable, and has a UI that makes sense. The software I use — TextMate, Safari 5, iTunes 10, iWork '09 — does everything I need without any of the cruft that's accumulated in modern equivalents.

I keep a newer Mac around for the occasional thing that won't run on Snow Leopard. That machine gets used maybe once a week. The old MBP gets used every day.

This site itself is written in plain HTML 4.01 and CSS. No CMS, no frameworks, no build tools. Just files in a folder. It's served from a basic host and has worked fine for nearly twenty years.

The Camera

I shoot film on an Olympus OM-1 — a fully mechanical 35mm SLR from the early 1970s. It needs no batteries to operate (a small battery only powers the light meter). If the electronics in modern cameras are going to rot in 20 years, the OM-1 will still be working fine.

My usual films are Kodak Portra 400 for colour and Ilford HP5 for black and white. I develop my own black and white film at home, which I'd recommend to anyone who hasn't tried it — it's cheaper than you'd think and surprisingly straightforward.

The constraint of 36 exposures per roll forces you to slow down and think before you shoot. That's not a limitation — it's the point.
